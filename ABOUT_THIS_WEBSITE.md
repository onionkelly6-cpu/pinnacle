# Pinnacle Legal & Business Law Website — What It Does (Plain-English Guide)

This explains what the website does, in everyday language. No coding
knowledge needed.

## The big picture

This is one website with three different "faces," depending on who's
looking at it:

1. **Anyone visiting the site** sees a normal law firm website — home
   page, about the firm, the types of cases they handle, lawyer bios,
   past case results, a contact form, and office locations.
2. **A client who has a case with the firm** can log in to their own
   private page and see how their case is going, download documents
   the firm has shared with them, and message their lawyer directly.
3. **The firm's own lawyers and staff** log in to a separate internal
   area where they manage every client's case, share documents, update
   case status, and handle new inquiries.

Think of it like online banking: the public website is like the
bank's homepage, the client area is like your personal account
dashboard, and the staff area is like the tools bank employees use
behind the scenes that customers never see.

## What happens when someone fills out "Contact Us"

1. A visitor fills out the contact form describing their legal
   situation.
2. That message doesn't create a case right away — it lands in an
   inbox the firm's staff and lawyers can see, listed as a new
   inquiry ("lead").
3. Someone at the firm reviews it and can mark it as "in progress"
   once they've reached out.
4. If it's a real case the firm is taking on, a lawyer clicks
   "Convert to Matter" — this one click sets up the client's file and
   opens their case in the system, all at once.
5. The lawyer then sets up a login for that client, so they can sign
   in and see their case.

## What the client sees once they log in

A simple dashboard showing:
- Every case the firm is handling for them, with its current stage
  (e.g., "Filed," "In Discovery," "Resolved").
- Any documents their lawyer has chosen to share with them —
  downloadable with one click.
- A message thread to communicate directly with their lawyer about
  the case.

All of this updates automatically. If their lawyer changes the case
status or shares a new document, the client sees it appear without
needing to refresh the page.

## What the firm's staff and lawyers see

A dashboard of every case, organized like a workload list:
- Lawyers can pick up new, unassigned cases.
- They can update a case's status as it moves forward.
- They can share documents with the client (or keep some
  internal-only, so the client never sees them).
- They can message the client.
- They can review new inquiries from the contact form and turn the
  promising ones into real cases.
- Lawyers can also set up logins for new clients or bring on other
  lawyers to the system.

Not everyone on staff can do everything — reception/admin staff can
handle new inquiries, but only lawyers can manage actual case files
and share documents, similar to how not every bank employee can
approve a loan.

## What's real right now, and what's still a work in progress

This website is still being built out step by step, so it's worth
being upfront about where things stand:

- **The case tracking, document sharing, messaging, and new-client
  intake all genuinely work** — a lawyer really can share a document
  or send a message, and the client really does see it appear.
- **The underlying storage is a temporary, working system**, not the
  firm's permanent database yet. Everything is saved and works
  correctly today, but a more permanent system will replace it later
  as the site matures — this is a completely normal stage for a
  website still being built.
- **Uploaded documents save the details** (file name, size, who
  shared it, who can see it) **but not yet the actual file itself** —
  that requires connecting the storage service that holds real files,
  which is planned but not switched on yet. Everything else about the
  document-sharing feature works as expected.
- **Automatic email notifications aren't sent yet** — e.g., a client
  won't currently get an email saying "your lawyer sent you a
  message," they'd need to log in and check. That's a planned next
  step.

## In short

The site already works as a real tool end-to-end: a stranger can
reach out, a lawyer can take them on as a client, share their case's
progress and documents, and message back and forth — all through the
website. A few pieces (permanent data storage, real file storage,
email alerts) are still being connected before this is ready for real
client data, but nothing about how it *works* today is fake or just
for show.
